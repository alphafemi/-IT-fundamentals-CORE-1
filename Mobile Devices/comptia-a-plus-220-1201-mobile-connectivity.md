## Overview

Mobile devices — phones, tablets, and wearables — require multiple methods of connectivity to communicate with computers, networks, and peripherals. These connections serve several purposes beyond simply charging:

- **Data synchronization** between device and computer
- **Backup** of device data
- **Device identification / authentication**
- **Internet sharing** with other devices

---

## Wired Connectivity

### USB Standards & Connector Types

USB (Universal Serial Bus) is the most common wired interface for mobile devices. Connector styles have evolved over time:

| Connector | Era | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **USB Type-A** | Legacy → Current | Standard host-side connector on PCs and laptops |
| **Mini USB** | Older mobile devices | Larger than micro; largely obsolete |
| **Micro USB (Micro-B)** | Older/mid-range devices | Very common on Android devices; still in use today |
| **USB-C** | Modern standard | Reversible, high-speed, multi-purpose |

> **Exam Tip:** Mini and Micro USB connectors are still found on devices in the field. Know the physical differences and their typical use cases.

### USB-C

USB-C is the modern standard connector moving forward and offers significant improvements over older USB types:

- **24 pins** inside a small, symmetrical connector
- **Reversible** — plugs in either orientation; no wrong way to insert
- **Backward compatible** — can carry USB 2.0 signals up through the latest USB specifications
- **Multi-protocol support** — a single USB-C port can carry:
  - USB data
  - DisplayPort video
  - HDMI video
  - Thunderbolt signals

> **Key Point:** USB-C is a **physical connector standard**, not a speed standard. The speed depends on the underlying USB specification (e.g., USB 3.2, USB4) or protocol being used.

### Lightning (Apple)

Older Apple iPhones and iPads use a proprietary connector called **Lightning** instead of standard USB.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Pins | 8 |
| Compatible devices | Older iPhone, iPad, iPod |
| Reversible | Yes — inserts in either orientation |
| Charging speed | Higher power output than older USB standards |

**Why Lightning was introduced:**
- Faster charging than older USB connections
- Reversible design for easier use
- Simpler, compact form factor across many Apple devices

> **Note:** Apple has been transitioning newer devices (iPad Pro, MacBook, and more recent iPhones) to USB-C as the industry moves toward a unified connector standard.

## Wireless Connectivity

### NFC (Near Field Communication)

NFC enables short-range wireless data transfer — typically **4 cm or less**.

**Common use cases on mobile devices:**

| Use Case | Example |
|---|---|
| Contactless payments | Tap to pay at a point-of-sale terminal |
| Access control | Using a phone or watch to open a door instead of a key card |
| Device-to-device transfer | Sharing a file or contact between two phones |
| Organizational authentication | NFC-enabled watch/phone replacing a physical ID badge |

NFC is commonly built into smartphones, tablets, and smartwatches.

### Bluetooth

Bluetooth provides **short-range, high-speed wireless networking** between personal devices, commonly referred to as a **PAN (Personal Area Network)**.

**Common use cases:**

- Wireless headphones and headsets
- Wireless keyboards and mice
- Smartphone tethering
- Connecting peripherals to tablets and phones

Bluetooth is designed for **personal/individual use**, though multiple devices can be connected simultaneously through pairing.

> **Exam Tip:** Bluetooth = PAN (Personal Area Network). NFC = very short range (≤4 cm), often for payments or authentication. Know the distinction.

### Hotspot & Tethering

A mobile phone can act as a wireless internet router to share its cellular data connection with other devices.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Hotspot** | Multiple devices connect to the phone for shared internet access (like a portable Wi-Fi router) |
| **Tethering** | A single device connects to the phone for internet access exclusively |

**Important notes:**
- Hotspot and tethering capabilities depend on both the **phone's software** and the **mobile carrier's plan**.
- Contact your mobile provider to confirm whether these features are available and if additional charges apply.

## Connector Comparison

| Connector | Pins | Reversible | Speed | Still Used? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mini USB | 5 | No | USB 2.0 | Rarely |
| Micro USB (Micro-B) | 5 | No | USB 2.0 | Yes (older devices) |
| USB-C | 24 | Yes | USB 2.0 → USB4 / Thunderbolt | Yes (modern standard) |
| Lightning | 8 | Yes | USB 2.0 equivalent | Yes (older Apple devices) |

## Key Takeaways

| Topic | Key Fact |
|---|---|
| USB-C | 24-pin, reversible, multi-protocol (USB, DisplayPort, HDMI, Thunderbolt) |
| Micro/Mini USB | Legacy connectors; still present on older devices in the field |
| Lightning | Apple proprietary; 8-pin; reversible; used on older iPhones/iPads |
| NFC | ≤4 cm range; used for payments, access control, device transfer |
| Bluetooth | PAN; short-range; headsets, keyboards, tethering |
| Hotspot | Multiple devices share cellular data via the phone |
| Tethering | Single device shares cellular data connection from phone |
| Mobile connections | Used for charging, sync, backup, and authentication — not just power |
