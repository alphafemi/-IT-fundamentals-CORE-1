## Overview

When crimping RJ45 connectors or punching down cables, you must follow a standardized pin-to-color mapping so that every cable communicates correctly. Two standards exist: **T568A** and **T568B**. Both are valid — but they must be used consistently. Mixing them causes connectivity failures.

---

## Wiring Standards Background

| Organization | Standard | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **ISO/IEC** | ISO/IEC 11801 | International cabling classification |
| **TIA (Telecommunications Industry Association)** | ANSI/TIA-568 | US commercial building cabling installation |

The **ANSI/TIA-568** standard defines:
- Which wires connect to which pins
- Which colors map to which pins
- The T568A and T568B color schemes

> **In practice:** T568B is the most commonly used standard in the United States. Most organizations pick one standard and apply it consistently throughout the entire installation.

---

## T568A vs. T568B Pin Assignments

Both standards use the same **RJ45 8-pin connector**. The difference is which color wire goes on which pin.

| Pin | T568A Color | T568B Color |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | White/Green | White/Orange |
| 2 | Green | Orange |
| 3 | White/Orange | White/Green |
| 4 | Blue | Blue |
| 5 | White/Blue | White/Blue |
| 6 | Orange | Green |
| 7 | White/Brown | White/Brown |
| 8 | Brown | Brown |

**Visual layout (looking into the RJ45 connector with the clip facing down, pin 1 on left):**

```
T568A:
Pin:  1          2        3           4      5         6       7           8
    Wht/Grn    Green   Wht/Org     Blue   Wht/Blu   Orange  Wht/Brn    Brown

T568B:
Pin:  1          2        3           4      5         6       7           8
    Wht/Org   Orange   Wht/Grn     Blue   Wht/Blu   Green   Wht/Brn    Brown
```

---

## How to Remember the Difference

**The shortcut:** Pins 4, 5, 7, and 8 are **identical** in both standards:

| Pin | Both Standards |
|---|---|
| 4 | Blue |
| 5 | White/Blue |
| 7 | White/Brown |
| 8 | Brown |

**Only memorize the differences — pins 1, 2, 3, and 6:**

| Pin | T568A | T568B |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | White/**Green** | White/**Orange** |
| 2 | **Green** | **Orange** |
| 3 | White/**Orange** | White/**Green** |
| 6 | **Orange** | **Green** |

In other words: **T568A and T568B swap the green pair and the orange pair** on pins 1, 2, 3, and 6.

> **Memory tip:** T568**B** = **B**usiness standard in the US. T568A tends to appear more in government/federal installations and in other countries.

## Identifying a Cable's Color Scheme

To determine which standard was used on an existing cable:

1. Hold the RJ45 connector with the clip facing **down** and pin 1 on the **left**
2. Look at the color of **pin 1**:
   - **White/Orange** on pin 1 → **T568B**
   - **White/Green** on pin 1 → **T568A**

**Example:** A cable shows the following left-to-right color order:
```
White/Orange | Orange | White/Green | Blue | White/Blue | Green | White/Brown | Brown
```
Matching to the table → this is **T568B**.

## Mixing Standards — What NOT to Do

**Never use T568A on one end of a cable and T568B on the other.**

- This creates a **miswired cable** that will not function correctly on a gigabit Ethernet network
- Some older references incorrectly call this a "crossover cable" — it is **not** a proper crossover cable
- Always use the **same standard on both ends** of every cable

**Consistency rule:** Once an organization adopts a standard (typically T568B in the US), all cables, patch panels, and keystone jacks in the building should use that same standard throughout.

## Keystone Jacks & Patch Panels

Keystone jacks (the modular connectors used in wall plates and patch panels) also follow T568A/B color coding.

**Important distinction:** The color labels printed on the keystone jack body **may not match T568A/B wire colors directly** — the jack's internal wiring routes the conductors to the correct RJ45 pins automatically. Follow the color labels printed specifically on the jack, not the T568 chart.

Many keystone jacks show **both A and B color schemes** side by side:
- **Top row** = T568A colors
- **Bottom row** = T568B colors

Simply punch each wire into the slot matching your chosen standard.

**Punch-down procedure:**
1. Select your standard (A or B) — match what's used everywhere else in the building
2. Match each wire color to the labeled slot on the jack
3. Use a punch-down tool to seat the wire and trim the excess
4. Maintain wire twists as close to the punch-down point as possible

## Key Takeaways

| Topic | Key Fact |
|---|---|
| ANSI/TIA-568 | US standard defining RJ45 pin/color assignments |
| T568B | Most common in the US; typical choice for new installations |
| T568A | Less common; used in some government and international installations |
| Pins 4, 5, 7, 8 | Identical in both standards (Blue, White/Blue, White/Brown, Brown) |
| Key difference | T568A and T568B swap the **green pair** and **orange pair** on pins 1, 2, 3, 6 |
| Pin 1 quick ID | White/Orange = T568B; White/Green = T568A |
| Never mix | Using A on one end and B on the other breaks connectivity |
| Keystone jacks | Follow labels printed on the jack — internal routing handles the rest |
| Consistency | One standard throughout the entire building installation |
