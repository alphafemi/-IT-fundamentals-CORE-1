# CompTIA A+ 220-1201 — Display Specifications

> **Exam Domain Reference** | Core 1 (220-1201) — Hardware (Domain 3)  
> Topics: Pixel density (PPI), refresh rate (Hz), resolution, aspect ratio, color gamut, sRGB, connection bandwidth

---



## Overview

Choosing the right display requires matching technical specifications to the intended use. A display for gaming has very different requirements than one for graphic design, lobby signage, or general office work. Understanding what each specification means — and how they interact — is essential for both the exam and real-world hardware selection.

---

## Pixel Density — PPI

**PPI (Pixels Per Inch)** measures how many pixels are packed into one linear inch of screen space. Higher PPI = sharper, crisper image.

**Formula:**
```
PPI = Total horizontal pixels ÷ Width of screen in inches
```

**Example — Same resolution, different screen sizes:**

| Display | Resolution | Width | PPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 27-inch 4K monitor | 3,840 px wide | ~24 inches | 3,840 ÷ 24 = **160 PPI** |
| 65-inch 4K TV | 3,840 px wide | ~57 inches | 3,840 ÷ 57 = **67 PPI** |

> **Key insight:** Two displays at the same resolution can have very different image clarity depending on their physical size. A 27-inch 4K monitor appears much sharper than a 65-inch 4K TV when viewed at the same distance.

**PPI vs. DPI:**
- **PPI** = screen pixels per inch (display specification)
- **DPI** = dots per inch (printer specification)
- When printing screen content, the printer's DPI must be sufficient to reproduce the image quality visible on screen

---

## Refresh Rate — Hz & FPS

The refresh rate describes how many times per second the display updates the image on screen.

- Measured in **Hertz (Hz)** — cycles per second
- Often equated to **Frames Per Second (FPS)** when V-sync (Vertical Sync) is enabled

**Common refresh rate use cases:**

| Refresh Rate | Common Use |
|---|---|
| 24 Hz / FPS | Film / cinema (standard movie frame rate) |
| 30 Hz / FPS | TV, online video |
| 60 Hz / FPS | Standard gaming, general use |
| 144 Hz+ | Competitive gaming, fast-action content |

**Why refresh rate matters:**
- Low refresh rate on fast-moving content → motion appears choppy or stuttery
- Higher refresh rate → smoother motion, especially for gaming and sports

> **Exam Tip:** The display's refresh rate is only one piece — the **video card** and the **connection type** must also support the same refresh rate. A 144 Hz monitor connected via an older cable that can't carry 144 Hz will be limited by the weakest link.

## Resolution

Resolution is the total number of pixels on a display, expressed as **width × height**.

**Common resolutions:**

| Name | Resolution | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **HD / 1080p** | 1,920 × 1,080 | Standard HD; very common |
| **1440p / QHD** | 2,560 × 1,440 | Mid-range; good balance |
| **4K / UHD** | 3,840 × 2,160 | High detail; 4× the pixels of 1080p |

Higher resolution on the same screen size = sharper image.

> **16:9 aspect ratio** is the standard across all these resolutions (e.g., 1920÷1080 = 16÷9).

---

## Aspect Ratio

The **aspect ratio** is the proportional relationship between a display's width and height.

| Ratio | Description | Common Use |
|---|---|---|
| **16:9** | Widescreen | Most modern monitors, TVs, laptops |
| **16:10** | Slightly taller | Some professional monitors |
| **21:9** | Ultrawide | Immersive gaming, multi-tasking |
| **4:3** | Square-ish | Legacy displays; older content |

Most modern content and displays use **16:9** as the standard.

## Color Gamut

**Color gamut** describes the range of colors a display can reproduce. The human eye can see a much wider range of colors than any current display can produce.

**Why it matters:**
- A wider color gamut = more accurate, vibrant, lifelike colors
- Critical for **graphic design, photography, and video editing**
- Less important for general web browsing or office use

### Common Color Standards

| Standard | Full Name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **sRGB** | Standard Red Green Blue | Most common; default for web and general use |
| **Adobe RGB** | Adobe RGB | Wider gamut than sRGB; used in professional photography |
| **DCI-P3** | Digital Cinema Initiatives P3 | Used in film production; wider than sRGB |
| **Rec. 709** | ITU standard for HDTV | Common in video production |

**How to compare:**
Manufacturers list color gamut as a **percentage of a standard** (e.g., "100% sRGB" or "98% DCI-P3"). Higher percentage = better coverage of that standard.

**Example comparison:**

| Display | sRGB Coverage | Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| Monitor A | 100% sRGB | Excellent for professional graphics work |
| Monitor B | 95% sRGB | Acceptable for general use; not ideal for color-critical work |

**Best color gamut options by technology:**
- **OLED** — generally best color gamut of any display technology
- **IPS LCD** — strong color gamut; some variants achieve near-OLED performance
- **TN LCD** — lowest color gamut of the three LCD types

## Connection Bandwidth Considerations

A display's full capabilities (resolution + refresh rate) can only be realized if the **connection between the GPU and the display** supports the required bandwidth.

**Key connection standards:**

| Connection | Version | Max Resolution | Max Refresh Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HDMI** | 2.1 | 4K | 144 Hz |
| **DisplayPort** | 2.1 | Dual 4K | 144 Hz each |

> **Important:** If you purchase a 144 Hz 4K monitor, your GPU and cable must both support HDMI 2.1 or DisplayPort 2.1. An older cable or port will bottleneck the display's performance.

**The full chain must be compatible:**
```
GPU (video card) → Cable → Display
All three must support the target resolution + refresh rate
```

## Specification Comparison Summary

| Spec | What It Measures | Higher = Better? | Key Consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPI | Sharpness / pixel density | Yes | Same resolution looks sharper on smaller screens |
| Refresh rate (Hz) | Smoothness of motion | Yes (for gaming/video) | GPU and cable must match |
| Resolution | Total pixel count | Yes | More pixels = sharper image |
| Color gamut (% sRGB) | Color accuracy/range | Yes | Critical for graphics; less important for general use |
| Aspect ratio | Width-to-height ratio | Depends on use | 16:9 is standard |
