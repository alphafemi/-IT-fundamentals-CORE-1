## SATA Overview

**SATA (Serial AT Attachment)** is the standard interface for connecting internal storage drives — HDDs and SSDs — to a desktop or laptop motherboard. It replaced the older PATA (Parallel ATA) standard and remains the dominant interface for 2.5-inch and 3.5-inch drives.

---

## SATA Versions & Speeds

| Version | Speed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SATA 1.0** | 1.5 Gbps | Original standard |
| **SATA 2.0** | 3 Gbps | Doubled from v1 |
| **SATA 3.0** | 6 Gbps | Current standard for most drives |
| **SATA 3.2** | 16 Gbps | Latest revision |

> **Memory shortcut:** SATA speeds roughly double with each major revision — 1.5 → 3 → 6 Gbps.

---

## SATA Connectors & Pinout

Each SATA drive has **two separate physical connectors:**

| Connector | Pins | Purpose | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data** | 7 pins | Data transfer to/from motherboard | L-shaped notch |
| **Power** | 15 pins | Power from power supply | Wider, L-shaped |

The data connector's **distinctive L-shape** prevents incorrect insertion.

Some older drives also include a **legacy Molex power connector** as an alternative to the SATA power connector — useful when connecting to an older power supply that lacks SATA power cables.

---

## SATA Topology

SATA is strictly **point-to-point (1-to-1)**:

- One data cable per drive → one motherboard port
- One power cable per drive → power supply
- **No daisy-chaining** — each drive needs its own dedicated connections
- Simple rule: **number of SATA ports on motherboard = maximum number of drives supported**

> **Example:** A motherboard with 6 SATA ports supports up to 6 storage drives.

Motherboards often color-code SATA ports (e.g., blue for SATA 2, black for SATA 3) to differentiate versions. Cables and ports are correspondingly color-matched.

## eSATA — External SATA

eSATA extends SATA to **external drives** outside the computer chassis.

| Feature | SATA (Internal) | eSATA (External) |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Inside the computer | Outside the computer |
| Signal | Same as eSATA | Same as SATA |
| Connector | 7-pin L-shaped | Different connector style |
| Max cable length | Short (internal routing) | ~2 meters |
| Interchangeable? | **No** | **No** |

> **Key exam point:** SATA and eSATA carry the **same signal** but use **physically different connectors** — you cannot use a SATA cable for eSATA, or vice versa.

eSATA ports may appear on the rear panel of a desktop or the side of a laptop. They can be mistaken for other port types — look closely at unfamiliar rear-panel connectors.

## Key Takeaways

| Topic | Key Fact |
|---|---|
| SATA | Standard internal storage interface; replaces PATA |
| SATA speeds | 1.5 → 3 → 6 Gbps (v1 → v2 → v3); v3.2 = 16 Gbps |
| Data connector | 7 pins; L-shaped |
| Power connector | 15 pins; wider L-shaped |
| Molex | Legacy power alternative on some older drives |
| SATA topology | Point-to-point; one cable per drive; no daisy-chaining |
| SATA port count | Equals maximum number of supported drives |
| eSATA | External SATA; same signal; different connector; ~2 m cable |
| SATA ≠ eSATA | Connectors are physically incompatible |
