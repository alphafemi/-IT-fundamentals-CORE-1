## Thermal Paper Replacement

Thermal printers use only thermal (thermochromic) paper — regular inkjet or laser paper will not work.

**Paper selection:**
- Must be the **correct size and type** for the specific printer make and model
- Thermal paper comes in rolls; sizes vary by printer

**Replacement process (typically under 1 minute):**
1. Load the new paper roll into the printer
2. Extend the paper to the outside of the printer
3. Close the printer cover over the paper
4. Lock/press down to secure

## Heating Element Cleaning

The heating element accumulates residue over time and may need periodic cleaning.

**Methods:**

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| **IPA (Isopropyl Alcohol)** swab | Most common; applied carefully to the element |
| **Cleaning pen** | IPA-filled pen for precise application and scrubbing |
| **Cleaning card** | Runs through the printer like paper; cleans the paper path and heating element simultaneously |

**Important:**
- Always check manufacturer documentation for the recommended cleaning method
- Heating elements and surrounding components are **sensitive** — use care when swabbing
- Never use harsh chemicals

## Debris Removal

Fine paper dust and debris accumulate inside any printer over time.

**Methods:**

| Method | Appropriate? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Compressed air** | Yes (portable printers taken outside) | Blow out debris; do outdoors to avoid spreading dust |
| **Damp cloth** | Yes (if printer can't be moved) | Wipe accessible interior surfaces |
| **Standard vacuum** | Avoid | Creates **static electricity** — dangerous to electronics |
| **Computer/printer-specific vacuum** | Yes | Anti-static design; safe for electronic equipment |

> **Exam Tip:** Standard vacuums generate static electricity — a hazard to sensitive electronic components. Use a purpose-built anti-static vacuum or compressed air instead.

## Heat Sensitivity & Output Preservation

Thermal output is vulnerable to heat sources and fades over time.

**Heat exposure causes darkening:**
- The thermochromic coating reacts to any heat — not just the printer's heating element
- Avoid placing thermal output near:
  - Car dashboards
  - Radiators
  - Hair dryers
  - Any other heat source

**Fading over time:**
- Thermal paper output gradually fades — receipts and labels may become unreadable after months to years
- For records that need long-term retention: take a **digital scan or photo** of the receipt/label, or print a copy on archival paper
