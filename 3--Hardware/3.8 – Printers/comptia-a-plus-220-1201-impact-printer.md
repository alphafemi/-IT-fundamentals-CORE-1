## How Impact (Dot-Matrix) Printers Work

A **dot-matrix printer** is an impact printer that creates output by physically pressing small **pins** against an **ink ribbon**, transferring ink to the paper behind it.

- Characters and images are formed from a **matrix of tiny dots**
- The print head moves back and forth across the page (one line at a time)
- The paper advances after each line is printed
- Because the printing physically **impacts** the paper, it can press through to create multiple copies simultaneously

**Common uses today:**
- Carbon copy / multipart forms
- Scenarios requiring physical impact on paper (carbon copies, receipts with duplicates)
- Some legacy industrial and point-of-sale environments

---

## Print Head

The print head contains a matrix of **pins** that press against the ribbon to create dots on the page.

- Moves **back and forth** horizontally across the paper
- Contains a **heat sink** on the back — print heads generate significant heat during operation
- Small and modular; the entire matrix is a single unit

To print a full page: the print head traverses each line horizontally, then the paper advances to the next line.

---

## Printer Ribbon

The ink ribbon is contained in a **cartridge** that spans the width of the print area.

- The ribbon is a **single continuous loop** — it cycles through continuously, rewinding and reusing until the ink is exhausted
- When the ribbon is depleted, **replace the entire cartridge**
- Ribbon cartridges are **model-specific** — must match the exact make and model of the printer
- Replacement is quick — pop out the old cartridge, pop in the new one

## Tractor Feed & Paper

Dot-matrix printers typically use **tractor feed** paper — continuous form paper with **holes along the left and right edges**.

- The holes align with **tractor feed sprockets** on the printer, pulling the paper precisely through the print path
- Holes on both sides must be **perfectly aligned** — misalignment causes paper jams
- Paper is usually **perforated** at regular intervals so it can be separated into individual pages
- **Perforation strips** on the edges can be removed, leaving a standard-looking page

**Common tractor feed paper types:**

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Standard continuous form | Blank paper on a continuous roll |
| Green bar paper | Alternating green and white stripes; used in legacy IT/programming environments |
| Multipart forms | Multiple layers for simultaneous copies |

---

## Multipart Paper

Impact printers excel at producing multiple copies simultaneously because the physical impact passes through multiple layers of paper.

**Technologies used:**

| Method | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **Carbon paper** | Traditional; a carbon sheet between pages transfers the impression |
| **Carbonless (NCR) paper** | Micro-encapsulated ink on the back of the top sheet reacts with clay coating on the sheet below to create a copy |

**Carbonless paper note:** The chemicals (ink + clay reaction) can be **skin irritants** — some users experience skin irritation after prolonged handling.

**Modern alternative:** Most organizations simply print multiple copies rather than using multipart forms — simpler, less expensive, and avoids the complexity of impact printer maintenance.

## Advantages & Disadvantages

| Aspect | Impact / Dot-Matrix |
|---|---|
| Cost per page | Low |
| Multipart copies | Yes — unique advantage over laser/inkjet/thermal |
| Noise level | High — among the noisiest printers |
| Output quality | Poor resolution; low image quality |
| Speed | Slow |
| Common today | Rare; niche legacy environments only |
