## Ribbon Replacement

The ribbon cartridge is a continuous loop of ink-soaked ribbon that cycles through the printer repeatedly.

**Symptom of depleted ribbon:** Output becomes progressively **lighter** until characters are difficult to read.

**Replacement process:**
1. Pop out the old ribbon cartridge (typically tool-free)
2. Snap the new cartridge into place
3. Resume printing

Total time: usually **under one minute**.

**Note:** Ribbon cartridges are model-specific — ensure the replacement matches the exact printer make and model.

## Print Head Replacement

The print head contains the matrix of pins that physically strike the ribbon and paper. Pins are mechanical and can wear out or break over time.

**Symptom of worn/failing print head:** One or more pins stop functioning → missing dots in characters; incomplete output.

**Safety first:** Print heads **get very hot** during operation. Allow the printer to cool before handling the print head.

**Removal methods vary by model:**
- **Screws** — some models require removing a few screws
- **Release lever / bar** — other models have a tool-free release mechanism

**Best practice:** When replacing the print head, also replace the ribbon cartridge at the same time — this gives the best possible output quality from the new print head.

## Paper Replacement & Alignment

Dot-matrix printers use **continuous form (tractor feed) paper** — a single long sheet with holes along both edges.

**Alignment is critical:**
- Tractor feed holes on **both sides** must align perfectly with the printer's feed sprockets
- Misalignment = paper jam or skewed output
- If printing on **pre-printed forms**, the form must be aligned precisely so printed content lands in the correct fields

**Paper path management:**
- Ensure nothing obstructs the paper **feeding into** or **exiting from** the printer
- Any constraint on the paper path will cause the paper to shift and jam

**Key difference from other printers:** There are no individual sheets — one continuous roll or stack feeds continuously through, so the entire paper path (incoming and outgoing) must remain clear at all times.
